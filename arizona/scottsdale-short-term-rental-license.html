Scottsdale Short-Term Rental License: The 2026 Compliance Guide

How to get and keep a Scottsdale short-term rental license: $250 a year per property, TPT first, neighbor notice, insurance, and penalties.

By Andrea Nava, former Airbnb Market Manager Verified against official sources 2026-08-25 11 min read

Scottsdale is one of the most competitive short-term rental markets in the country and one of the most actively enforced. The city licenses every property rented for less than 30 days, lets anyone verify your license status publicly, and attaches minimum fines to specific failures — $1,000 for operating without a license, $1,000 for a missed guest background check, $500 for missing insurance (City of Scottsdale, Information for Owners and Operators; Scottsdale STR ordinance).

The good news: the process is finite. Five steps, one $250 fee per property per year, and a handful of habits. Everything below was verified against City of Scottsdale and State of Arizona sources on August 25, 2026. This is general information, not legal or tax advice — confirm with the city and a licensed professional before acting.

Who needs a license

If you offer a Scottsdale property for rent for less than 30 days, you need a city license for that property and must maintain it annually. Owners renting for 30 days or longer do not (City of Scottsdale). Scottsdale Ordinance 4566 is the underlying authority and also carries the safety, health, and neighborhood notification requirements (City of Scottsdale).

Two structural rules matter before you list. Residential use only: Ordinance 4719 defines "event center" specifically to strengthen enforcement against STRs used for weddings, corporate events, and large parties (City of Scottsdale). Occupancy is capped: all dwelling units, including STRs, have a maximum family size of six adults and their related dependent children, and the dwelling plus any accessory guest house must be rented together, not independently (City of Scottsdale).

The annual fee

Renewal application fee $250 per property, non-refundable
License term One year from date of issuance unless suspended or revoked
Transferability Not transferable as to location or person

Sources: City of Scottsdale and the Scottsdale STR ordinance, verified August 25, 2026. State law caps a local STR license fee at the actual cost of issuance or $250, whichever is less (A.R.S. § 9-500.39).

The five-step licensing process

Scottsdale publishes the process in this order, and the order matters because Step 2 needs the output of Step 1. What you submit is short: owner or agent contact details, property address, proof of TPT license, and emergency contact information (City of Scottsdale; Licensing Process Guide, PDF).

  1. Get an Arizona TPT license at AZTaxes.gov — required for each property, needed for the city application, and Scottsdale asks you to list Scottsdale (SC) as a region code.
  2. Apply for the Scottsdale license for each property.
  3. Notify neighbors and provide proof of insurance, then complete the attestation and upload proof of coverage.
  4. Complete Maricopa County requirements. Each property must be registered with the Maricopa County Assessor under A.R.S. § 33-1902 before it is occupied — the city states plainly that it is illegal to rent before registering.
  5. Generate and display the required short-term rental notice as defined in Sec. 18-175(h).

The ordinance also requires an attestation that the owner and each designee is not a registered sex offender and has no qualifying felony conviction in the past five years, an agreement to comply with applicable laws, and — for individual applicants — proof of lawful presence (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

State law sets the clock: a city must issue or deny an STR license within seven business days of receiving the required information, and may deny only for narrow reasons such as missing information, unpaid fees, false information, a currently suspended license for the property, or a disqualifying criminal history (A.R.S. § 9-500.39).

The emergency contact requirement

This is the requirement that generates the most avoidable fines. Scottsdale requires a designated emergency contact who is a real person with a 24-hour phone number, and on request from public safety personnel that contact must respond in person at the property within one hour. An "emergency" includes any call for service where police or fire respond, including a nuisance party. Minimum fines: $500 for failing to respond to the physical location, $250 for failing to arrive within one hour, and $500 if the owner intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly causes the contact to fail to comply — and out-of-date contact information can be evidence of the owner's state of mind (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

The city notes that one of its most common complaints is a lack of response from property managers or emergency contacts (City of Scottsdale). If your contact lives in another state, you do not have a compliant contact. You have a liability.

Notification to adjacent properties

Within 30 days of receiving your Scottsdale license — and any time property contact information changes — you must notify all single-family residential properties adjacent to, directly across the street from, and diagonally across the street from the property; in a multi-family building, all units on the same floor. The letter must include the license number, the rental's physical address, and the emergency contact's name, address, and 24-hour telephone number. Then complete the city's attestation and upload proof of liability insurance (City of Scottsdale). Failing to notify carries a fine of at least $500, reducible to $100 once you show compliance (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

Insurance

Scottsdale's ordinance requires every short-term or vacation rental to be insured with liability coverage of at least $500,000, either directly or through an online lodging marketplace (City of Scottsdale). Failure to maintain it is a fine of at least $500, reducible to $100 after you show coverage was obtained (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

Guest background checks

No later than 24 hours before every stay, the owner or designee must check the person booking against the U.S. Department of Justice National Sex Offender Public Website, retain the printout for 12 months after the booking date, and produce it for the city on demand. The requirement is satisfied or waived if the marketplace where the rental is advertised performs the check (Scottsdale STR ordinance), a waiver state law requires (A.R.S. § 9-500.39).

The mandatory minimum fine for failing the background check or record retention requirement is $1,000, as is knowingly booking on behalf of a sex offender who will not be staying, and the court may not suspend any part of those fines (Scottsdale STR ordinance). If you take direct bookings, this record is yours to keep.

The posted notice, pool barriers, and advertising

Posted notice. Sec. 18-175(h) requires a notice in 14-point or larger bold font, laminated, displayed inside the front door and at the primary backyard door, listing the location of fire and life safety equipment, Scottsdale nuisance waste disposal regulations, the local contact's name and 24/7 phone number, the city license number, and the prohibited uses and occupancy limit (City of Scottsdale; Scottsdale STR ordinance).

Pool barriers. Scottsdale has pool barrier requirements for STR properties with a pool, hot tub, or spa (City of Scottsdale); the city's owner guide points to Scottsdale Revised Code §18-175(g), §31-16, and Chapter 31, Article XIII, and tells owners to keep barriers in working order (Good Neighbor Guide for Owners and Managers, PDF).

Advertising. Every listing, including Airbnb and Vrbo, must show the Scottsdale license number, the occupancy limit, and the restriction on non-residential uses such as corporate events, large parties, and weddings (Good Neighbor Guide for Owners and Managers, PDF). Failing to display the license number in each advertisement is a fine of at least $500, reducible to $100 after you comply (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

Renewal and maintaining the license

The license runs one year from issuance, and a separate current license must be maintained for each dwelling unit at the same $250 non-refundable fee (Scottsdale STR ordinance). Between renewals, keep contact information current with the city and the neighborhood: update the license, then re-notify neighbors and file the attestation (City of Scottsdale).

Source: Scottsdale STR ordinance, consistent with the state penalty tiers in A.R.S. § 9-500.39. Unless timely appealed, a civil penalty is due within 30 days of the city's initial notice.

Suspension. The city must initiate suspension proceedings for three verified violations within 12 months — excluding aesthetic, solid waste, or parking violations that are not also serious health and safety threats — or for one serious verified violation: a felony at or near the property by the owner or designee; serious injury or wrongful death from the owner's or designee's knowing, intentional, or reckless conduct; knowingly housing a sex offender, allowing adult-oriented or prostitution offenses, or operating a sober living home; or knowingly allowing event-center, retail, restaurant, or banquet use. A final suspension lasts one year, reducible by a hearing officer to six months if the owner has taken substantial steps to prevent recurrence (Scottsdale STR ordinance).

Taxes on a Scottsdale STR

All Scottsdale STRs carry TPT and transient tax liability, collected by the State of Arizona. The city states it imposes a 1.75% transaction privilege tax and a 5% transient tax on vacation and short-term rentals (City of Scottsdale). ADOR's table effective August 1, 2026 lists Scottsdale's hotels rate (code 044) at 1.70%, the additional hotel/motel tax (code 144) at 5.00%, and the Maricopa County transient lodging rate including the state portion (code 025) at 7.27% (ADOR rate table, 8/1/2026). Because the city page and state table differ slightly on the city rate, verify your combined rate with ADOR's rate look-up tool before pricing.

Your Scottsdale compliance checklist

  1. Arizona TPT license obtained, with Scottsdale (SC) region code listed.
  2. Scottsdale license application filed for each property, $250 paid.
  3. Emergency contact named — real person, 24-hour phone, on site within one hour.
  4. $500,000 liability insurance in force; proof uploaded.
  5. Neighbor letters sent within 30 days of license issuance; attestation filed.
  6. Maricopa County Assessor registration completed before occupancy.
  7. Required notice in 14-point bold, laminated, posted at both doors.
  8. Pool, spa, or hot tub barriers installed and maintained.
  9. Listings show the license number, six-adult limit, and no-events restriction.
  10. Background check process for direct bookings, records kept 12 months.
  11. Renewal calendared; contact changes trigger city update plus new neighbor letters.

Common mistakes I see

  • Applying before the TPT license exists. The city needs the TPT number, so an early application just stalls.
  • Notifying neighbors and stopping there. The attestation and insurance upload are separate steps, and the fine attaches to the failure, not the intent.
  • A remote emergency contact. One hour, in person, on request. Hire local.
  • Advertising that omits the occupancy limit or license number. The missing license number alone carries a $500 minimum.
  • Marketing the property as a wedding or event venue. Ordinance 4719 exists for exactly that, and it is a single-violation suspension path.
  • Forgetting the county. Renting before Maricopa County Assessor registration is, in the city's words, illegal.

Once the license, insurance, notice, and neighbor letters are handled, Scottsdale goes back to being what it actually is: a crowded market where the difference between a strong year and a mediocre one is how well your listing competes for attention. Quick answers

Frequently asked

Who needs a Scottsdale short-term rental license?

Any owner or operator offering a Scottsdale property for rent for less than 30 days must obtain and maintain an annual city license for each property. Owners who rent only for 30 days or longer do not need the city short-term rental license.

How much is the Scottsdale short-term rental license fee?

The annual license fee is $250 per property, and the renewal application fee is also $250. Both are non-refundable. The city states the fee revenue goes entirely toward the cost of licensing vacation and short-term rentals.

Do I need a TPT license before applying in Scottsdale?

Yes. Step 1 of the city's process is obtaining a transaction privilege tax license from the Arizona Department of Revenue. A valid TPT license is required for each rental property, and the TPT number is needed to apply for the Scottsdale license. Scottsdale also asks owners to list Scottsdale (SC) as a region code on the license.

How does neighbor notification work in Scottsdale?

Within 30 days of receiving your license, or any time property contact information changes, you must notify all single-family residential properties adjacent to, directly across, and diagonally across the street from the property, or in a multi-family building, all units on the same floor. The letter must include the STR license number, the property address, and the name, address, and 24-hour telephone number of the emergency point of contact. You then file an attestation with the city.

Does Scottsdale require sex offender background checks on guests?

Yes, unless your booking platform performs the check. No later than 24 hours before every stay, the owner or designee must screen the person booking through the U.S. Department of Justice National Sex Offender Public Website and retain the printout for 12 months, available to the city on demand. The mandatory minimum fine for non-compliance is $1,000.

What can get a Scottsdale short-term rental license suspended?

Three verified violations within a 12-month period, excluding aesthetic, solid waste, or parking violations that are not also serious threats to public health and safety, or a single serious verified violation such as a felony committed at the property by the owner or designee, knowingly housing a sex offender, or knowingly allowing the property to be used as an event center or banquet space. A final suspension lasts one year, reducible to six months if the owner shows substantial steps to prevent recurrence.
